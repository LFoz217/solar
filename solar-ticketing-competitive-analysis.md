## Executive Summary

The UK event ticketing market is highly fragmented, with platforms ranging from global giants (Ticketmaster, AXS) to UK-native challengers (Skiddle, FIXR, Fatsoma). Most platforms suffer from at least one of these critical weaknesses:

- **Opaque or high fee structures** that erode organiser and buyer trust
- **Post-event payout models** that damage promoter cashflow
- **Poor customer support** at volume
- **Closed ecosystems** that limit organiser data ownership
- **App or platform lock-in** limiting reach

Fee transparency, pre-event cashflow access, superior organiser tooling, and genuine buyer protections represent the clearest differentiators available to Solar.

### 1. Eventbrite

**Overview:** Global market leader for self-service event ticketing. Dominant in the UK for professional, corporate, and community events. Went public in 2018; significant brand recognition.

#### Fee Structure

| Fee Type | Amount (UK) |
|---|---|
| Service fee (paid tickets) | **6.95% + £0.59 per ticket** |
| Payment processing | Included in above (no separate charge) |
| Free events | £0 |
| Organiser platform fee | £0 (included in service fee) |

The 6.95% + £0.59 fee is all-in — Eventbrite handles payment processing within this rate. Organisers can choose to absorb the fee or pass it to buyers. There is no additional Stripe/card fee on top.

> ⚠️ **Critical gotcha:** Since 2023, if an organiser cancels an event and refunds attendees, **Eventbrite keeps all service fees**. On 500 tickets at £25, that's approximately £1,162 in non-refundable fees lost by the organiser.

#### Key Features — Organisers

- **Check-in:** Eventbrite Organiser mobile app with QR code scanning, manual attendee lookup, real-time attendance tracking, at-the-door sales
- **Analytics:** Attendee summary reports, sales by ticket type, traffic/conversion dashboards, real-time sales data
- **Embeds:** Checkout widget for embedding on external websites
- **White-label:** Not offered as standard. No full white-label option.
- **Ticket types:** Multiple paid/free tiers, early bird, hidden/invite-only, donations, group tickets
- **Capacity management:** Waitlists, ticket release waves
- **Email tools:** Attendee messaging via platform; limited email marketing (Mailchimp integration)

#### Key Features — Buyers

- **Digital tickets:** QR code tickets via email and app
- **Resale:** No built-in resale. Buyers directed to third parties.
- **Wallet support:** Apple Wallet and Google Wallet pass support

#### Payout Terms

Default payout begins **5 days after event ends**, arriving in the organiser's bank account **5–7 business days later** (so 10–12 days post-event effectively). Faster schedules available: weekly (Wednesdays) or twice monthly (1st and 15th) for qualifying accounts. No pre-event payouts.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- Customer support is widely criticised as non-existent for small/medium organisers — automated responses, 200-character limit on support tickets, no phone support
- Accounts randomly locked due to MFA rollouts without warning
- Fees kept on cancellations is a major pain point
- No white-label offering
- No built-in ticket resale
- Slow payouts with no pre-event funding option
- Trustpilot and PissedConsumer ratings are extremely low (1.4/5 from ~1,000+ reviews)
- Charges for email marketing features that were previously free
- Known for hosting fraudulent events with limited recourse for buyers

### 2. Ticketmaster

**Overview:** The world's largest ticketing company, owned by Live Nation. Dominates stadium and arena events in the UK. Not designed for independent promoters or small events. Acquired many sub-brands (Universe, TicketWeb) for different market segments.

#### Fee Structure

Ticketmaster's pricing is **not publicly disclosed** — all fees are negotiated individually with venues and promoters. Typical buyer-facing fees in the UK include:

| Fee Type | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Service fee (buyer) | ~15–25% of face value |
| Order processing fee | £1.75–£4.00 per order |
| Venue/box office levy | Variable (passed through to venue) |
| Organiser commission/deal | Negotiated; Ticketmaster often takes a guaranteed % of gross |

Dynamic pricing (surge pricing for high-demand events) was introduced controversially — came under UK investigation following Oasis ticket sales in 2024. Fees reportedly surged 41% across the UK live events market in 2024 according to consumer monitoring data.

#### Key Features — Organisers

- **TM1 platform:** Self-service event creation tool (claims <8 minutes to launch)
- **Universe:** Lighter-weight platform for smaller/mid events, also owned by Ticketmaster
- **Check-in:** Hardware and app-based scanning infrastructure at scale
- **Analytics:** Extensive sales reporting, demographic data, fan intelligence
- **White-label:** Enterprise-grade white-label and API integration available for large clients
- **Capacity management:** Full arena/stadium seating charts, holds and kills, section management
- **Integrations:** CRM, venue management systems, hardware box offices

#### Key Features — Buyers

- **Digital tickets:** App-based (Ticketmaster UK app), with transfer and selling capabilities
- **Resale:** Fan-to-Fan resale and Verified Resale marketplace at face value cap (UK law compliance)
- **Wallet support:** Apple Wallet, Google Wallet

#### Payout Terms

Negotiated per contract. Established promoters may receive pre-event advances or regular settlements. No standard public payout schedule.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- Completely inaccessible to small or independent promoters — requires enterprise-level relationship
- Notorious for high fees from buyer perspective; faces ongoing regulatory scrutiny in UK and US
- Dynamic pricing caused major public backlash (Oasis 2024 controversy)
- Antitrust scrutiny in US — DOJ lawsuit ongoing
- Exclusive venue deals lock out competing platforms
- Very poor perception among fans (fees, scalpers, dynamic pricing)
- App experience criticised for being slow and crash-prone during on-sales

### 3. DICE

**Overview:** London-founded mobile-first ticketing platform focused on music, clubs, and cultural events. Positions itself as the anti-Ticketmaster — no scalping, transparent pricing, anti-touting ethos. Operates in UK, US, Europe, and Australia. Backed by SoftBank.

#### Fee Structure

DICE's fee model is intentionally opaque, which is ironic given its anti-scalping brand positioning. Known information:

| Fee Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Face value retained by organiser | Negotiated (varies per deal) |
| Booking fee added to buyer | ~20–25% on top of face value (2024 example: $25 ticket → $31.16 charged to buyer) |
| Fee split | Shared between DICE and the promoter/venue; exact split not disclosed |
| Organiser platform access | Free; revenue from booking fee share |

> ⚠️ DICE claims fees are "split between DICE and the event organiser" but multiple artists and promoters have publicly complained that they see none of the booking fee revenue, creating significant distrust.

#### Key Features — Organisers

- **Creator tool:** Self-service event management dashboard
- **Analytics:** Built-in insights on show performance and audience behaviour
- **Distribution:** Events surface in DICE's discovery feed (curated by DICE editorial team — not all events approved)
- **Check-in:** DICE scanning app
- **Curation barrier:** DICE acts as a gatekeeper — not all applicants are accepted onto the platform, and events require editorial approval

#### Key Features — Buyers

- **Digital tickets:** App-only (no printable tickets, no browser checkout)
- **Resale:** Waitlist-based returns — returned tickets go back to fans on the waitlist at face value (no scalping)
- **Wallet support:** Tickets in-app only; no Apple/Google Wallet export
- **Cool-off period:** Brief window to cancel after purchase (unique feature)

#### Payout Terms

Not publicly documented. Assumed post-event settlement based on deal terms negotiated per event.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- **App-only** — no web checkout is a significant conversion barrier, especially for older audiences
- Editorial gatekeeping means many promoters can't access the platform
- Fee distribution is opaque — promoters don't know exactly what they receive
- Customer service for refunds is reportedly slow and automated (3.5 months without refund complaints reported in 2025)
- No printable tickets — some venues and audiences find this limiting
- Limited discoverability outside the DICE app
- Not self-service — requires approval and onboarding

### 4. FIXR

**Overview:** UK-born platform (founded 2012, London) focused on nightlife, student events, and festivals. Strong in university markets. Self-service and plug-and-play with powerful promoter tools. Growing rapidly as a Fatsoma competitor.

#### Fee Structure

| Fee Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Organiser fee | **Free** |
| Buyer booking fee | Low % (not publicly disclosed); reduced rates for student and charity events |
| Payment processing | Included or passed through (not specified publicly) |

FIXR does not publish exact booking fee percentages on its public website. Internal quotes suggest fees are competitive with or below Fatsoma (~5%). Student event rates are explicitly lower.

#### Key Features — Organisers

- **Check-in:** Entry Manager app — group scan (admit multiple with one scan), real-time capacity across multiple devices
- **Analytics:** FIXR Insights — audience demographics (location, age, gender, spending habits), predictive AI identifying high-value fans and churn risk, real-time event data, marketing attribution
- **Marketing tools:** Message Centre (targeted emails and push notifications), ticket shop customisation
- **Ticket types:** Multiple tiers, early bird, student pricing, charity pricing
- **Embeds:** Ticket shop embeddable on external websites
- **Capacity management:** Real-time capacity dashboard across multiple entry points
- **Guest list management:** Built-in

#### Key Features — Buyers

- **Digital tickets:** QR code via app and web
- **Resale:** Not prominently featured (limited info)
- **Wallet support:** App-based tickets

#### Payout Terms

Not publicly disclosed. Based on user reports, standard post-event settlement.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- Exact fee structure not disclosed publicly — creates distrust for new organisers
- Less name recognition than Eventbrite or Skiddle for non-student events
- Limited marketplace/discovery for buyers compared to Skiddle or DICE
- Payout terms not transparent

### 5. Fatsoma

**Overview:** Manchester-based platform popular with nightlife promoters and student events. Free for organisers; revenue from buyer booking fees. Has a network of resellers/reps built into the model. Competes directly with FIXR and Skiddle.

#### Fee Structure

| Fee Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Organiser fee | **Free** |
| Buyer booking fee | ~**5% + 49p per ticket** |
| Payment processing | Stripe charges applied separately |
| Organiser option | Can absorb fee or pass to buyer |

> ⚠️ **Drip pricing complaint:** Multiple users report a hidden "order processing fee" added at checkout, separate from the disclosed booking fee, with no way to avoid it — a known dark pattern.

#### Key Features — Organisers

- **Check-in:** Fatsoma Scan app (free, barcode scanning on smartphones, real-time attendance)
- **Analytics:** Sales dashboard, audience insights, buying trend data
- **Reseller/Rep network:** Built-in rep system — promoters can create a network of resellers who earn commission for selling tickets (unique differentiator for nightlife)
- **Marketing tools:** Social media integration, audience building, direct marketing to followers
- **Ticket types:** Multiple tiers, payment plans
- **Embeds:** Not prominently advertised
- **Capacity management:** Basic

#### Key Features — Buyers

- **Digital tickets:** QR code via app and web
- **Resale:** No official resale; platform says it "cannot offer advice on reselling"
- **Wallet support:** App-based

#### Payout Terms

> ⚠️ **Major red flag:** Fatsoma retains funds for **up to 5 months** after the event before automatically paying out to the organiser's bank account. This is the longest hold in the market and a serious cashflow problem for promoters. If valid bank details aren't on file at payout time, the funds are permanently retained by Fatsoma.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- 5-month payout hold is extraordinary and a major competitive weakness
- Drip pricing / hidden fees at checkout
- Stripe payment processing is separate, meaning actual total buyer cost is higher than advertised
- Limited discovery features — buyers must find events through external promotion
- No resale functionality
- Customer service complaints

### 6. Skiddle

**Overview:** UK's largest independent ticketing and event listing platform. Founded in Lancashire (2001). Strong marketplace of buyers — events listed on Skiddle benefit from organic discovery. Popular for clubbing, festivals, and live music events. Free for organisers; revenue from buyer booking fees.

#### Fee Structure

| Fee Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Organiser fee | **Free** |
| Buyer booking fee | **10% + 25p per ticket** (min. £1) |
| Payment processing | Included |
| Alternative model | Promoters can pay the booking fee themselves (becomes a commission deducted from face value) |

At 10%, Skiddle has one of the **higher buyer fees** in the UK market, particularly compared to Fatsoma (5% + 49p) and Ticket Tailor (flat fee).

#### Key Features — Organisers

- **Check-in:** RapidScan app — QR scanning, real-time attendance tracking, ticket validation
- **Analytics:** Promotion Centre dashboard, real-time sales data, remittance reports, buying trend insights
- **Resale:** Re:Sell — organiser-enabled face-value resale for sold-out events; buyers receive automated face-value refund when ticket is resold
- **Marketing tools:** E-flyers, push notifications, SMS alerts, newsletters, optimised landing pages
- **Ticket types:** Multiple tiers, payment plans, group booking
- **Event Series:** Tool for recurring events — create a year's worth of listings quickly
- **Session-based ticketing:** For experiences with fixed time slots
- **Capacity management:** Real-time via RapidScan
- **Discovery:** Events listed in Skiddle's marketplace — significant organic buyer reach

#### Key Features — Buyers

- **Digital tickets:** App and web QR tickets
- **Resale:** Re:Sell (face value resale when organiser enables it)
- **Cool:Off:** Limited cancellation window
- **Wallet support:** Not prominently advertised
- **Waitlists:** Queue system for sold-out events

#### Payout Terms

Remittance runs **every Tuesday and Thursday at 11am and 4pm** — funds arrive in the organiser's bank account **2–3 days later**. Payouts are triggered **after** the event; minimum payout threshold is **£50**. Events grossing under £50 carry over to the next cycle.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- 10% buyer fee is among the highest in the market — can deter price-sensitive buyers
- Post-event payout only (no advance or pre-event payment)
- £50 minimum threshold means small events wait longer
- No white-label offering
- No organiser-facing subscription model or priority support tier
- Marketplace is UK-focused — limited international reach

### 7. See Tickets

**Overview:** Major UK ticketing company (owned by Vivendi). Predominantly used for large music, theatre, and festival events. Not self-service for independent promoters — requires a commercial relationship. Significant buyer-facing brand.

#### Fee Structure

Fees are **individually negotiated** with each venue/promoter. Not publicly disclosed. Buyer fees are event-specific and displayed at checkout. The booking fee exists to cover "24/7 booking service, labour, credit card commissions, and operating costs."

Some West End theatre events carry no booking fee (the £1 shown is a theatre restoration levy, not a See Tickets fee).

#### Key Features — Organisers

- Box office integration
- Box office on-the-door sales support
- Reporting and reconciliation tools
- Dedicated account management for large clients

#### Key Features — Buyers

- Digital tickets via email/app
- Print-at-home option
- Fan-to-fan resale (event dependent)

#### Payout Terms

Negotiated per contract.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- Not accessible to small/independent promoters
- No published pricing — requires direct commercial negotiation
- Not self-service
- Legacy technology compared to newer platforms

### 10. Ticket Tailor

**Overview:** UK-based platform with a radically different model: flat-fee pricing per ticket (not percentage-based). Popular with community events, arts organisations, charities, and small businesses who sell enough tickets to make the economics work in their favour versus percentage-based competitors.

#### Fee Structure

| Fee Type | Amount |
|---|---|
| Organiser fee (pay-as-you-go) | **£0.60 per ticket** (flat fee) |
| Organiser fee (pre-paid credits) | As low as **£0.22 per ticket** (500-credit bundle = £205) |
| Buyer booking fee | Optional — organiser chooses to pass on or absorb |
| Payment processing | Separate: Stripe, PayPal, or Square (charged directly by processor) |
| Free events | **Always free** |
| Charities/non-profits | Discounted rates |

**Key advantage:** At higher ticket prices, Ticket Tailor's flat fee is dramatically cheaper than percentage-based platforms. A £100 ticket on Eventbrite costs £7.54 in fees; on Ticket Tailor, the organiser pays £0.60.

#### Key Features — Organisers

- **Embeds:** Full widget embed for own website
- **Ticket types:** Unlimited ticket types, donation options, discount codes
- **Check-in:** Organiser app with QR scanning
- **Analytics:** Basic sales dashboard
- **White-label:** Near white-label — organisers can host checkout on their own domain
- **Integrations:** Mailchimp, Zapier, Google Analytics

#### Key Features — Buyers

- Email/web QR tickets
- Apple/Google Wallet support

#### Payout Terms

Stripe/PayPal/Square pay out directly on their standard schedules — typically **2–7 days**, not controlled by Ticket Tailor. This means organisers can receive funds faster than on most platforms.

#### Notable Limitations & Complaints

- No built-in buyer marketplace/discovery — buyers need to be driven to the event page
- No resale functionality
- The separate payment processing fee means real costs are higher than the headline fee suggests
- Less suited to events relying on discovery from a marketplace
- No phone support

## Market Gaps & Solar Opportunities

The competitive research reveals several significant, exploitable gaps in the current UK ticketing market. These represent Solar's clearest opportunities for differentiation.

---

### 🔴 Gap 1: Pre-Event Cashflow — Nobody is Solving This Well

**The problem:** Every major platform in the UK pays organisers *after* the event. Fatsoma waits up to 5 months. Skiddle runs twice-weekly remittance with a £50 minimum. Eventbrite takes 10–12 days post-event. For promoters covering deposits on artists, venues, and production weeks in advance, this is a major operational problem.

**The opportunity:** Solar could offer **rolling pre-event payouts** (e.g. weekly from ticket on-sale date), or even a **revenue advance product** (similar to Ticket Fairy Capital) that gives promoters access to projected revenue before the event. Even offering payouts from the moment of sale — rather than post-event — would be a significant differentiator. This directly addresses the number one complaint from promoters in the UK market.

---

### 🔴 Gap 2: Fee Transparency Is Universally Broken

**The problem:** DICE hides its fee split. FIXR doesn't publish rates. Fatsoma uses drip pricing. TicketSource quotes one rate, Stripe charges separately. Ticketmaster doesn't publish anything. Even Eventbrite — the most transparent — buries the cancellation fee-keeping policy.

**The opportunity:** Solar could build an entire brand position around **radical fee transparency** — a real-time fee calculator on the homepage, clear breakdowns of what every party receives per ticket, and a contractual commitment to never charging hidden fees. The market data shows trust in ticketing platforms is at an all-time low (2024). A platform that makes fees legible and fair has a genuine emotional and functional edge.

---

### 🟠 Gap 3: No Platform Offers a Good Organiser-Facing Subscription Model

**The problem:** Most platforms are free for organisers but make their money from buyer-facing booking fees. This creates misaligned incentives — higher booking fees mean less buyer conversion, but the platform doesn't care because it's the organiser who loses ticket sales. No major platform offers a **paid organiser subscription in exchange for lower or zero buyer fees**.

**The opportunity:** Solar could offer a tiered model: free tier with standard buyer fees, and a paid organiser plan (e.g. £49/month) with reduced buyer fees, advanced analytics, priority support, and white-label tools. This creates predictable revenue, aligns platform incentives with organiser success, and attracts higher-volume promoters who want to reduce buyer friction.

---

### 🟠 Gap 4: White-Label Is Underserved for Mid-Market Organisers

**The problem:** White-label ticketing exists but is only accessible to enterprise clients (Ticketmaster, AXS, See Tickets) or requires significant technical setup. Eventbrite, DICE, Fatsoma, Skiddle, and FIXR all offer zero white-label capability. Ticket Tailor is near-white-label but lacks a marketplace.

**The opportunity:** Solar could offer **affordable, self-serve white-label ticketing** for mid-market promoters — venues, festivals, recurring club nights — at a subscription price point (rather than an enterprise contract). This gives organisers brand ownership and first-party data ownership, which is increasingly valuable as third-party cookies disappear.

---

### 🟠 Gap 5: DICE's App-Only Model Is a Real Conversion Barrier

**The problem:** DICE requires buyers to download and use the app. There is no web checkout. This creates friction — particularly for older audiences, casual buyers, or anyone who doesn't want to install another app. It also limits the types of events DICE is suitable for.

**The opportunity:** Solar could position its mobile experience as best-in-class while offering full web checkout — capturing buyers that DICE loses. If Solar also builds a discovery feed and curated experience (without the gatekeeping), it could compete for the music/culture audience DICE owns.

---

### 🟡 Gap 6: Resale Is Poorly Handled Across the Market

**The problem:** Eventbrite has no resale. Fatsoma has no resale. FIXR has no resale. Billetto has no resale. Skiddle's Re:Sell is organiser-opt-in and only activates on sold-out events. DICE's waitlist-based model works but is app-locked. The secondary market remains dominated by touts and secondary platforms (Viagogo, StubHub) that erode trust in the primary ticketing experience.

**The opportunity:** Solar could build a **native, face-value-capped resale marketplace** — allowing ticket holders to list tickets for resale through Solar, with face value protection enforced. This keeps the entire buyer journey within Solar's ecosystem and is something most UK platforms currently fail to offer.

---

### 🟡 Gap 7: Organiser Support Is Universally Terrible

**The problem:** Eventbrite is notorious for poor support (automated responses, no phone line, 200-character limit). DICE takes months to process refunds. Fatsoma has limited support. Skiddle has a phone line but it's not 24/7.

**The opportunity:** A genuine **dedicated account management model** — even at scale — would be a competitive differentiator. This could be tiered (chat for free tier, phone/Slack for paid subscribers, dedicated account manager for enterprise). In a market where everyone complains about being ignored, basic responsiveness becomes a brand differentiator.

---

### 🟡 Gap 8: Payment Plans for Buyers Are Rare

**The problem:** Only Skiddle and Fatsoma offer buyer payment plan options. With the cost-of-living crisis continuing to affect UK consumers, the ability to split ticket payments across multiple months (especially for festivals and multi-day events) is increasingly demanded.

**The opportunity:** Solar could offer a **buy now, pay later** option for buyers (partnering with Klarna, Clearpay, or building natively), allowing larger-ticket events to increase conversion and reach audiences who might otherwise not purchase. This is standard in retail but almost unheard of in UK ticketing.

---

### 🔵 Strategic Positioning Summary for Solar

Based on this analysis, the following positioning is recommended:

**Target segment:** Independent and mid-market UK promoters (nightlife, festivals, live music, culture) who are currently using Skiddle, Fatsoma, Eventbrite, or FIXR and are frustrated by payout timing, fee opacity, or lack of tools.

**Core differentiation:**
1. Rolling payouts from day of sale (or pre-event advances)
2. Radically transparent fee structure — published, calculable, guaranteed
3. Full white-label for mid-tier promoters at affordable price
4. Web + app checkout (no app required to buy)
5. Native face-value resale

**Secondary differentiation:**
1. Genuine human customer support
2. BNPL for buyers
3. Advanced AI-powered analytics comparable to FIXR Insights but more accessible

**Pricing strategy:** Match or undercut Skiddle (10% + 25p) and Eventbrite (6.95% + £0.59) at the standard tier, but offer a paid organiser subscription that brings buyer fees below Fatsoma (currently lowest at 5% + 49p). Position Ticket Tailor's flat-fee model as an inspiration — but combine it with a genuine marketplace that Ticket Tailor lacks.
